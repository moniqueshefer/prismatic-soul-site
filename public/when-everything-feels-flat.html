A practice

When Everything Feels Flat

Dimmed, distant, going through the motions. A graded way to invite the system back up — starting with something that costs nothing.

11:34 · Flat → up · Free, no sign-up

For when you’re flat, dimmed, going through the motions — when there’s nothing to calm because nothing’s running. A graded way to invite the system back up: everyone starts at the same gentle door, and climbs as far as they have capacity for today. Also for anyone listening on behalf of someone who has gone quiet and far off.

Listening note — sitting is best for this one — a chair is perfect. Eyes open or closed to start; we’ll open them near the end. Nothing here asks you to produce energy you don’t have, and every step is optional once you’ve begun.

Transcript

So everything feels flat. Maybe not dramatically — just dimmed. The colour turned down, the day gone a little grey, you going through the motions a half-step behind yourself. Or further off than that: turned right down, the world at a distance, as though it, or you perhaps, are happening behind glass. Hard to feel much, hard to care much, hard to want anything in particular.

I want to say one thing first, before we do anything at all. This is allowed. You are not lazy, and you have not failed at something. This flatness is your system being economical — it has pulled the energy back and turned the dial down to save resources. That is a very ancient and intelligent thing for a body to do. We are not going to scold it for doing so.

And if you’re here for someone else — someone who has gone quiet and far off, and you want to help — you’re welcome to do this alongside them, or do it for yourself, so you can be the calm, warm presence in the room. That is its own kind of help.

This practice is not going to try to force change upon you. You have probably had plenty of people — possibly including yourself — telling you to cheer up, push through, snap out of it. But I’m sure you’ve noticed that doesn’t work, and it isn’t kind, because there is nothing here to push through with. A flat system has almost no fuel. So we won’t spend energy you don’t have. The plan is to invite a little back, slowly, from the edges in. And we’ll start with one tool that works even when the tank is near empty, because it doesn’t cost energy — it makes it. Your own voice. Before you run away, please understand we are not singing here.

We’ll start with a mumbled hum. Not a song, not for anyone else’s ears; we’re not trying to sound nice, we’re not even trying to be audible. Start with the smallest hum you can make, barely audible inside your own head. A quiet “mmm” on the exhale — the kind you might make without meaning to.

Try one now, whenever you’re ready. Let it be tiny.

Good. While you do it, I’ll tell you why it works. A hum does two opposite things at once. The long sound on the out-breath settles you — the body reads it as safety. And the making of the sound brings you up — it switches on the part of your nervous system built for being seen, for being here, in the room. Settling you and building you up, in the same breath. It is the one thing that can lift a flat system without asking it to spend anything.

Let the next one be just slightly longer. Let it drop a little deeper. Feel for where it buzzes — the lips, maybe the chest feeling the slightest bit of vibration. Let it be plain, even ugly; the ugliness is fine. It keeps you clear of any urge to produce or perform — that would spend resources, and we’re trying to bank them.

A few more on your own now. Keep it slow.

Now notice — has the hum put a little back? A degree or two of presence, a bit more here than there was a few minutes ago. If it has, we’ll ask for a little more. And if it hasn’t, we are going to invite you to rest right here — the hum was enough for today, and that is no small thing. Stay as long as you like.

If you’ve built up a little to re-invest, we are going to start at the edges. Wiggle your fingers. Wiggle your toes. Bring some life back to the far ends of you. Rub your palms together until they’re warm, and rest them somewhere — your face, the back of your neck. Warmth is a signal too. Pause to feel the good sensation deeply, and take the time to specifically acknowledge and welcome it, with each touch.

Now we change the breath. Until now we leaned on the out-breath, which settles. To come up, we lean the other way — on the in-hale, which rouses. Where a long exhale calms a system down, a fuller in-breath draws energy in.

So, we follow with a handful of brighter in-breaths. Breathe in through the nose a little fuller and a little quicker than usual, and let the out-breath simply fall out, soft, no effort. The in-breath is the one you’re reaching for.

On the inhale, visualise the air coming in charged with a soft white glow. We are telling the body to interpret the inhale as a resource... which of course it is.

In... and let go. In... and let go.

Five or six like that, gently, no more. If you feel at all light-headed, just stop and let the breath settle back to ordinary — that’s the sign you’ve had plenty.

Now if you are ready, a gentle seated movement to bring you a little more into your body.

Fold slowly forward over your lap. Let the head go heavy and hang, the arms drop toward the floor. Rest there a moment, folded — the head below the heart, which the body finds quietly reassuring.

Now the part that rouses. Roll yourself back up, slowly, from the very base of the spine, stacking one bone at a time, the head coming up last and heaviest. Take your time — rising slowly keeps the head clear. As you come up and your chest opens to the room, notice that the rising itself is the lift. Folding lets you relax; coming up brings you up.

Twice more, at your own pace. Fold, rest, and unfurl back to sitting tall.

Now let your eyes do the last of it. If they’ve been closed, let them open; if they were open, let them lift — a flat system stares at nothing, or stares inward.

Let the room back in. Slowly turn your head, and let your eyes move around the actual space you’re in. Don’t look for anything in particular; just let the room arrive. Find one real thing — a corner, a window, the light somewhere. Then another. Let your eyes rest on it a moment.

You were far off, and now here is the room. Here is where you are.

Now notice where you are. Most likely something has moved in you — more here than when you started, a little warmer, the world a touch closer than it was behind the glass. Maybe a lot, maybe only a degree, and either is completely all right. You met yourself exactly where you were, which is the hardest and kindest thing, and you brought yourself some caring, nurturing support by your own quiet effort. That counts.

Where you are right now is not who you are. It’s the weather — and flat weather lifts. You don’t have to be all the way back; a few degrees warmer is the practice working.

Keep these nearby — the hum, the brighter breath, the acknowledgement of sensation, the roll-up — for whenever the dial has dropped low. They’re yours, and they bring you more than they cost.

Let it be enough for now.
